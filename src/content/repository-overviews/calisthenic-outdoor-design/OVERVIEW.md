## A backyard exercise idea became a design package that could be checked before anyone builds it.

This project began with a physical space and two very different users: an adult and a young child sharing an outdoor calisthenics area. A model of bars alone would not answer whether the layout, grips, falls, materials, and foundations belonged together. I used Blender to work out the shape, then turned that shape into dimensions, research notes, and preliminary engineering checks that another professional could review.

## Following the design beyond the render

The proposed layout combines pull-up, snake, dip, and five-zone monkey bars with impact-absorbing flooring. Different heights and fall zones affect the spacing and floor specification. The documents move from visual models to tube sections, load paths, connections, foundations, and a preliminary bill of materials. They also explore a practical assembly problem: large fully welded structures are awkward to transport, while field welding can compromise corrosion protection. The proposed route uses shop-welded, galvanized sections joined by bolts on site.

The calculations are first-order checks, so the package calls out places where assumptions are especially consequential: the high monkey-bar brace, cantilevered grips, lateral stability, welds, anchors, and flooring. The Thai and English handover sets make those questions visible to different readers. The written specifications govern the proposed dimensions; the images help explain them.

The render shows the intended arrangement, not a completed installation. This repository is a **draft design and research package**. It does not contain a certified structural calculation or evidence that the gym was built. A licensed engineer and builder would need to verify the final design, materials, site, and construction before use, especially by a child.
